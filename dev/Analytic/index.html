Analytic geometry and the Legendre basis

Bernstein ellipses

RigorousInvariantMeasures.EllipseCompatibility — Type
EllipseCompatibility

What certify_compatibility proves: the map dynamic (a single map or a family with interval parameters) is compatible with the geometry G, every inverse branch maps $E_R$ into $E_r$, and $\sup_{E_R}\sum_j|τ_j'| \le W$. R_image is the certified lower bound on the Bernstein parameter of the images $F_j(\partial E_r)$; it exceeds G.R.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.EllipseGeometry — Type
EllipseGeometry(r, ρ, R)

The ellipse parameters $1 < r < ρ < R$ of an analytic basis, in the chart $t = 2x - 1$: functions are bounded on $E_r$, the strong norm is that of $E_ρ$, and the transfer operator is to map functions analytic on $E_r$ to functions analytic on $E_R$. The values are stored as the Float64s passed, exactly. Two geometries built from the same numbers are different objects, and the bases and certificates built on one are not accepted with the other. Whether a map is compatible with the geometry is proved by certify_compatibility.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.certify_compatibility — Method
certify_compatibility(G::EllipseGeometry, D::PwMap; n = 1024, cover = 64)
    -> EllipseCompatibility

Proves that the full-branch map D is compatible with G, branch by branch, or throws an ArgumentError. The branches may contain Interval parameters: every enclosure below then holds for every map of the family, and one call certifies all of them. With $F_j(t) = 2T_j((t+1)/2) - 1$ the branch in the global chart:

  1. D is full branch (is_full_branch);
  2. on a cover of the closed ellipse $E_r$ by cover × cover boxes (those whose Bernstein parameter can be $\le r$), $\operatorname{Re}T_j'$ has the sign of the branch;
  3. the Bernstein parameter of $F_j(\partial E_r)$, enclosed on n arcs by bernstein_expansion, is $> R$.

From 2, on the convex set $E_r$, $T_j' \ne 0$ and $F_j$ is injective (Noshiro–Warschawski), so $F_j$ is a homeomorphism of the closed $E_r$ onto a domain bounded by the Jordan curve $F_j(\partial E_r)$. By 3 that curve lies outside $E_R$; $E_R$ is connected and contains $[-1,1] = F_j([t_j, t_{j+1}])$ by 1, so it lies in $F_j(E_r)$, and $τ_j = F_j^{-1}$ maps it into $E_r$. Then $|τ_j'(z)| = 1/|T_j'(τ_j(z))| \le 1/\min_{E_r}|T_j'|$, and since $T_j'$ is zero-free on $E_r$ the minimum modulus principle puts that minimum on $\partial E_r$, where it is enclosed on n arcs: $W = \sum_j 1/\min_{\partial E_r}|T_j'|$, rounded up.

The two boundary enclosures are those of bernstein_geometry, which looks at $\partial E_r$ only; check 2 is what makes them valid inside.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.search_ellipse_geometry — Method
search_ellipse_geometry(D::PwMap, rs; cost = (r, R_image, W) -> R_image / r,
                        n = 1024, cover = 64) -> NamedTuple

Tries each $r > 1$ in rs for the full-branch map D and returns the one that maximises cost among those that pass the two checks of certify_compatibility on $E_r$:

  • $\operatorname{Re}T_j'$ has the sign of the branch on the cover of $E_r$ by cover × cover boxes, for every branch;
  • R_image > r, where R_image is the certified lower bound on the Bernstein parameter of the images $F_j(\partial E_r)$ enclosed on n arcs, minimised over the branches.

For these $r$, W is the certified upper bound $\sum_j 1/\min_{\partial E_r}|T_j'|$ of certify_compatibility, and cost(r, R_image, W) is called; the default is $R_\mathrm{image}/r$. The result has fields r, R_image, W, cost for the best $r$, and table, one named tuple (r, R_image, W, feasible, cost) per entry of rs, in order (R_image = W = NaN and cost = -Inf where the derivative check fails, cost = -Inf where R_image ≤ r). Throws an ArgumentError if no $r$ passes.

The search chooses $r$ and certifies nothing by itself. Any $ρ, R$ with $r < ρ < R < R_\mathrm{image}$ then pass certify_compatibility(EllipseGeometry(r, ρ, R), D) with the same n and cover, which is the certificate.

Strips and annuli

RigorousInvariantMeasures.AnnulusCompatibility — Type
AnnulusCompatibility

What certify_compatibility proves for an AnnulusGeometry G and a lift lift of degree degree: every inverse branch maps $S_R$ into $S_r$, and $\sup_{S_R}\sum_j|τ_j'| \le W$. R_image is the certified lower bound on $|\operatorname{Im} T|$ on the boundary lines of $S_r$; it exceeds G.R.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.AnnulusGeometry — Type
AnnulusGeometry(r, ρ, R)

The strip half-widths $0 < r < ρ < R$ of a Fourier analytic setting: functions are bounded on $S_r = \{|\operatorname{Im} x| < r\}$, the strong norm is that of $S_ρ$, and the transfer operator is to map functions analytic on $S_r$ to functions analytic on $S_R$. In the variable $z = e^{2πix}$ the strip $S_η$ is the annulus $e^{-2πη} < |z| < e^{2πη}$. The values are stored as the Float64s passed, exactly; as for EllipseGeometry, two geometries built from the same numbers are different objects. Whether a map is compatible with the geometry is proved by certify_compatibility.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.certify_compatibility — Method
certify_compatibility(G::AnnulusGeometry, T; degree, n = 1024, cover = 64)
    -> AnnulusCompatibility

Proves that the circle map with lift T is compatible with G, or throws an ArgumentError. T must accept Complex{Interval} and Taylor1 of them, and the statement assumes what no finite computation can check: T is analytic on a neighbourhood of the closed strip $\bar S_r$ and $T(x + 1) = T(x) + d$ with $d =$ degree $\ne 0$. The routine checks

  1. $T(1) - T(0)$ encloses $d$ (a consistency check of the assumption, not a proof);
  2. on the cover of the period rectangle $[0,1] \times [-r, r]$ by cover × cover boxes, $\operatorname{Re}T'$ has the sign of $d$;
  3. on n arcs of each boundary line $\operatorname{Im} x = \pm r$, $0 \le \operatorname{Re} x \le 1$, the enclosure of $|\operatorname{Im} T|$ is $> R$; the minimum of its lower bounds is R_image.

Since $T'$ is 1-periodic, 2 holds on all of $\bar S_r$, and $T$ is injective there, $\bar S_r$ being convex (Noshiro–Warschawski). $T(x) - dx$ is 1-periodic, hence bounded on $\bar S_r$, so $\operatorname{Re} T(z) \to \pm\infty$ as $\operatorname{Re} z \to \pm\infty$ uniformly in the strip. Then $T(S_r) \cap S_R$ is open in $S_R$ ($T$ is open), closed in $S_R$ (a limit $T(z_k) \to y \in S_R$ has $\operatorname{Re} z_k$ bounded, so a subsequence $z_k \to z \in \bar S_r$ with $T(z) = y$, and $z \notin \partial S_r$ by 3), and contains the real line. As $S_R$ is connected, $S_R \subset T(S_r)$, and the $|d|$ inverse branches $τ_j(y) = T^{-1}(y + j)$, $j = 0, \dots, |d| - 1$, map $S_R$ into $S_r$. By 2, $1/T'$ is holomorphic, bounded and 1-periodic on $\bar S_r$, so in the variable $z = e^{2πix}$ it is holomorphic on a closed annulus and its maximum modulus is on the boundary lines, where it is enclosed on the same n arcs: $|τ_j'(y)| = 1/|T'(τ_j(y))| \le 1/\min_{\partial S_r}|T'|$, and $W = |d| / \min_{\partial S_r}|T'|$, rounded up.

Remark. Check 3 needs T on the boundary path only, not on a box of the plane. If T extends to complex intervals and $T'$ is computed with TaylorSeries, the image of the arc $x + i r$, $x \in I_i = [x_i, x_{i+1}]$, is contained in

\[T(x_i + i r) + T'(I_i + i r)\,[0,\, x_{i+1} - x_i],\]

since $T(x + ir) - T(x_i + ir) = \int_{x_i}^{x} T'(t + ir)\,dt$ and the average of $T'$ on the segment lies in the box $T'(I_i + ir)$, which is convex; the same holds on $\operatorname{Im} x = -r$. The routine uses the direct evaluation of T on $I_i \pm ir$ instead.

The Legendre basis

RigorousInvariantMeasures.Legendre — Type
Legendre{T<:AbstractFloat, G} <: Basis

The orthonormal Legendre basis of $L^2([0,1], dx)$ up to degree N, $e_k(x) = (2k+1)^{1/2}P_k(2x-1)$, $k = 0, …, N$, with $P_k$ the Legendre polynomials, $\|P_k(2\cdot-1)\|_{L^2(dx)}^2 = 1/(2k+1)$. The projection onto the span is the $L^2(dx)$-orthogonal one, $\Pi_N$, so $\|\Pi_N\| = 1$ and $\int\Pi_Nf = \int f$. A coefficient vector $b$ stands for $\sum_k b_k e_k$ and its $L^2(dx)$ norm is $\|b\|_2$. Under $t = 2x - 1$ the basis is the orthonormal basis $\tilde P_k = (2k+1)^{1/2}P_k$ of $L^2([-1,1], dx/2)$, so every matrix computed here is also the matrix in that convention.

T is the floating-point type of the certified output (Float64 or BigFloat).

Two constructors:

  • Legendre(N; T = Float64): the basis alone, geometry = nothing;
  • Legendre(N, G::EllipseGeometry; T = Float64): the basis built on the ellipse geometry G, which it stores. The geometry holds no dynamic.

galerkin_matrix and l2_resolvent_bound_average_zero need only the map. galerkin_matrix_from_chebyshev, legendre_dfly and legendre_dfly_smallest_M need the geometry and the EllipseCompatibility of the map with it. The basis does not take part in DiscretizedOperator, whose assembly goes through preimages and dual elements.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.change_of_basis_matrices — Method
change_of_basis_matrices(Bc::Chebyshev, Bl::Legendre) -> (cheb_to_leg, leg_to_cheb)

cheb_to_leg * c encloses the orthonormal Legendre coefficients $b$ of $f = \sum_m c_mT_m(2x-1)$, and leg_to_cheb * b the Chebyshev coefficients of $\sum_k b_ke_k$. Both are BallMatrix{T}, upper triangular, with the entries of chebyshev_to_legendre_rational and legendre_to_chebyshev_rational scaled by $(2k+1)^{\mp1/2}$ and enclosed by outward rounding.

Throws an ArgumentError unless both bases are built on an EllipseGeometry, the same object, and have the same degree.

For an operator, cheb_to_leg * Q * leg_to_cheb is the matrix in the Legendre basis of the operator that Q represents in the Chebyshev basis. For the interpolatory Chebyshev matrix that operator is $\Pi^{\mathrm{Cheb}}_NL$, not the Galerkin $\Pi_NL$; for the latter see galerkin_matrix_from_chebyshev.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.chebyshev_to_legendre_rational — Method
chebyshev_to_legendre_rational(N) -> Matrix{Rational{BigInt}}

The exact matrix $C$ with $T_n = \sum_{k\le n} C_{k+1,n+1}P_k$ for $0 \le n \le N$: column n+1 holds the Legendre coefficients of $T_n$. It is upper triangular and the inverse of legendre_to_chebyshev_rational.

Computed by $T_{n+1} = 2xT_n - T_{n-1}$ with $xP_k = ((k+1)P_{k+1} + kP_{k-1})/(2k+1)$, in exact rational arithmetic. The closed form (Alpert and Rokhlin 1991) is $C_{n+1,n+1} = \sqrt\pi/(2\Lambda(n))$ on the diagonal for $n \ge 1$ and, for $k < n$ with $n - k$ even, $C_{k+1,n+1} = -n(k+\frac12)\Lambda(\frac{n-k-2}2)\Lambda(\frac{n+k-1}2)/((n+k+1)(n-k))$; the test suite compares the two. An internal helper, as the one above.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.evaluate — Method
evaluate(B::Legendre, i, x)

$e_{i-1}(x) = (2i-1)^{1/2}P_{i-1}(2x-1)$, by the three-term recurrence $(n+1)P_{n+1}(t) = (2n+1)tP_n(t) - nP_{n-1}(t)$. For an Interval argument the recurrence runs in interval arithmetic and the result encloses the value; interval arithmetic does not see the cancellation in the recurrence, so the radius grows geometrically with the degree and a high degree wants Interval{BigFloat}.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.galerkin_matrix — Method
galerkin_matrix(B::Legendre, D::PwMap; nsub = B.N + 8, order = max(48, 3B.N ÷ 4 + 16), κ = 4,
                precision = max(256, 3B.N + 128)) -> BallMatrix

Certified enclosure of the matrix $A_{j+1,k+1} = \langle Le_k, e_j\rangle_{L^2(dx)}$, $0 \le j, k \le N$, of $\Pi_NL\Pi_N$ in the orthonormal basis, with $L$ the transfer operator of D on $L^2(dx)$. By duality $\langle Le_k, e_j\rangle = \int_0^1 e_k(x)\,e_j(T(x))\,dx = \sum_b\int_{I_b}e_k(x)\,e_j(T_b(x))\,dx$, the sum over the branches of D, so no inverse branch is needed.

Each branch domain $I_b$ is split into nsub subintervals at the points $a + (b-a)(1 - \cos(\pi i/\mathrm{nsub}))/2$, finer near the ends, where $e_j\circ T$ grows fastest off the real line. On a subinterval with centre $c$ and half-length $\le H$, in the variable $s = (x - c)/H \in [-1, 1]$:

  1. the Taylor coefficients of order $\le d$ (order) of $p_k(s) = e_k(c + Hs)$ and $q_j(s) = e_j(T_b(c + Hs))$ are computed in Interval{BigFloat} at precision bits (the recurrence of evaluate, on Taylor polynomials; $T_b$ evaluated on a Taylor1), and $\int p_k^{\le d}q_j^{\le d}$ is integrated exactly against the moments $\int s^m$;
  2. the remainders are bounded by Cauchy's estimate on the disc $|s| \le κ$: for $g$ analytic there with $|g| \le M$, the Taylor coefficients satisfy $|g_i| \le Mκ^{-i}$, so $\sup_{|s|\le1}|g - g^{\le d}| \le Mκ^{-(d+1)}/(1 - 1/κ)$. $M$ comes from $|P_n(u)| \le ρ(u)^n$, $ρ(u)$ the Bernstein parameter of $u$ (bernstein_parameter), evaluated on a box containing the disc and on the image of that box under $2T_b - 1$. The bound $|P_n| \le ρ^n$ on $E_ρ$: the Chebyshev coefficients of $P_n$ are nonnegative and sum to $P_n(1) = 1$ (Lemma lem:legendre-cheb of appendix-legendre.tex), and $|T_m| \le (ρ^m + ρ^{-m})/2 \le ρ^m$ on $E_ρ$;
  3. $|\int(PQ - pq)| \le |I|\,(R_k\sup|Q_j| + \sup|p_k|\,S_j)$, with $R_k$, $S_j$ the two remainder bounds and $|I|$ the length of the subinterval.

Where an endpoint of $I_b$ is an interval of nonzero width (a branch point of a mod1_dynamic), the integral over the uncertain part, of length at most that width, is bounded by the width times $\sup|e_k||e_j\circ T_b|$ over it. The branches must accept Taylor1{Interval{BigFloat}} and Complex{Interval{BigFloat}} arguments.

The subintervals run in parallel on Threads.nthreads() threads. The output is a BallMatrix{T} for B::Legendre{T}, rounded outward from the BigFloat enclosure. Row 1 encloses $(1, 0, …, 0)$, since $\int Lf = \int f$.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.galerkin_matrix_from_chebyshev — Method
galerkin_matrix_from_chebyshev(B::Legendre{T}, D::PwMap, C::EllipseCompatibility; Nprime,
                               ϵ, max_iter) -> BallMatrix{T}

Certified enclosure of the same matrix as galerkin_matrix, from the Chebyshev interpolation coefficients of $LT_m$, $m \le N'$, that assemble returns for Chebyshev(Nprime, Eρ(r); T) (an extension method: using FFTW first, and using GenericFFT as well for BigFloat), at the working precision of T (Float64, or BigFloat at the current precision, for which D should be built at T = BigFloat so that its branch points are enclosed at that precision; ϵ and max_iter are passed to the preimage search). Nprime must be a power of two with $N' \ge N$. B must be built on an EllipseGeometry G = (r, ρ, R) and C must be certify_compatibility(G, D) for this D (both checked, by identity); its W and the geometry give:

  • $\sup_{E_R}|Le_k| \le W\sup_{E_r}|e_k| \le W(2k+1)^{1/2}r^k =: M_k$ ($|P_k| \le r^k$ on $E_r$, as in galerkin_matrix);
  • the Chebyshev coefficients of $g = Le_k$ satisfy $|c_0| \le M_k$, $|c_m| \le 2M_kR^{-m}$ (Trefethen, ATAP, Theorem 8.1);
  • the interpolant in the $N'+1$ Chebyshev points has coefficients $\tilde c_m = c_m + \sum_{l\ge1}(c_{2lN'-m} + c_{2lN'+m})$ for $0 < m < N'$, $\tilde c_0 = c_0 + \sum_{l\ge1}c_{2lN'}$ and $\tilde c_{N'} = c_{N'} + \sum_{l\ge1}c_{(2l+1)N'}$ (ATAP, Theorem 4.2), so for $0 \le m \le N'$, $|\tilde c_m - c_m| \le 2M_k(R^m + R^{-m})R^{-2N'}/(1 - R^{-2N'})$;
  • the tail $g - \sum_{m\le N'}c_mT_m$ has $L^2(dx)$ norm at most its sup norm, at most $\sum_{m>N'}|c_m| \le 2M_kR^{-(N'+1)}/(1 - R^{-1})$, which bounds its pairing with each $e_j$.

Then ``\langle Lek, ej\rangle = \sum{m\le N'}C{j+1,m+1}c_m/(2j+1)^{1/2}

  • \langle\mathrm{tail}, e_j\rangle$, with$C`` of

chebyshev_to_legendre_rational. The $T_m$ with $N < m \le N'$ are kept: they have Legendre components of degree $\le N$, so truncating the Chebyshev matrix at $N$ would give $\Pi^{\mathrm{Cheb}}_NL$ and not $\Pi_NL$.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.l2_resolvent_bound_average_zero — Method
l2_resolvent_bound_average_zero(A::BallMatrix, z = 1) -> (; bound, Rbar)

For A the Galerkin matrix of galerkin_matrix, an upper bound bound on $\|(zI - A_0)^{-1}\|_2$, where $A_0 = A[2:\mathrm{end}, 2:\mathrm{end}]$ is the matrix of $\Pi_NL\Pi_N$ on the mean-zero subspace $V_0 = \mathrm{span}\{e_k : k \ge 1\}$, and $\bar R = \max(1, \mathrm{bound})$. The bound is svd_bound_L2_opnorm_inverse of BallArithmetic on the ball $zI - A_0$, valid for every matrix in the ball; it is Inf when the certified smallest singular value is not positive.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.l2_transfer_norm_bound — Method
l2_transfer_norm_bound(D::PwMap; tol = 1e-3) -> Float64

An upper bound on $\|L\|_{L^2(dx)\to L^2(dx)}$ for the transfer operator of D: $\|L\|^2 \le \sup_y\sum_b|τ_b'(y)| \le \sum_b\sup_{I_b}1/|T'|$, since by Cauchy–Schwarz $|Lf|^2 \le (\sum_b|τ_b'|)(\sum_b|f\circ τ_b|^2|τ_b'|)$ and the second factor integrates to $\|f\|^2$. Each supremum is branch_inverse_derivative (global optimisation in interval arithmetic) and the result is $(\sum_b\cdot)^{1/2}$ rounded up.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.legendre_dfly_l1 — Method
legendre_dfly_l1(B::Legendre, C::EllipseCompatibility, M; T = Float64) -> NamedTuple

Constants of a Lasota–Yorke inequality for $\|\cdot\|_ρ$ with the $L^1$ norm in the second term, $\|Lf\|_ρ \le A_M\|f\|_ρ + B'_M\|f\|_{L^1}$, $A_M = τ_MC_2$, $B'_M = \sum_{j\le M}(2j+1)ρ^j$, as intervals, for the geometry of B and the W of C (checked as in legendre_dfly).

Pending the paper's lemma

This inequality is not yet stated in the Diffusion paper; the constants follow the derivation below and are to be checked against the lemma once it is written. Split $Lf = (I - \Pi^{\mathrm L}_M)Lf + \Pi^{\mathrm L}_MLf$. The first term is bounded by lem:legendre-tail and $\|Lf\|_R \le C_2\|f\|_ρ$. For the second, the orthonormal Legendre coefficients are $b_j = \langle Lf, \tilde P_j\rangle = \langle f, \tilde P_j\circ T\rangle$, so $|b_j| \le (2j+1)^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^1}$ since $|P_j| \le 1$ on $[-1,1]$, and $\|\Pi^{\mathrm L}_MLf\|_ρ \le \sum_{j\le M}|b_j|(2j+1)^{1/2}ρ^j$ as in the proof of lem:legendre-cheb.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.legendre_to_chebyshev_rational — Method
legendre_to_chebyshev_rational(N) -> Matrix{Rational{BigInt}}

The exact matrix $L$ with $P_n = \sum_{k\le n} L_{k+1,n+1}T_k$ for $0 \le n \le N$: column n+1 holds the Chebyshev coefficients of $P_n$. It is upper triangular, and $L_{k+1,n+1} = 0$ unless $n - k$ is even.

Computed by the Legendre recurrence $(n+1)P_{n+1} = (2n+1)xP_n - nP_{n-1}$, with $xT_0 = T_1$ and $xT_k = (T_{k+1} + T_{k-1})/2$ for $k \ge 1$, in exact rational arithmetic. The closed form, with $\Lambda(z) = \Gamma(z+1/2)/\Gamma(z+1)$, is $L_{k+1,n+1} = \frac{2 - \delta_{k0}}{\pi}\Lambda(\frac{n-k}2)\Lambda(\frac{n+k}2)$ for $n - k$ even (Alpert and Rokhlin 1991, SIAM J. Sci. Stat. Comput. 12, 158–179); the test suite compares the two. The same matrix relates the shifted families $P_k(2x-1)$ and $T_k(2x-1)$ on $[0,1]$. An internal helper: the basis-level interface is legendre_basis and change_of_basis.
