The transfer operator at a point and on an interval

Jets of $Lf$ at a point or uniformly on an interval, and Taylor models of $Lf$ on an interval (the latter in the TaylorModels extension: load it with using TaylorModels).

RigorousInvariantMeasures.transfer_jet — Method
transfer_jet(D::PwMap, f, Y, k; ϵ = 0.0, max_iter = 100) -> Taylor1

Certified Taylor jet of order k of $Lf$ on the point or interval Y: for every $y \in Y$ and $m = 0, \dots, k$ the coefficient of $t^m$ contains $(Lf)^{(m)}(y)/m!$, where $(Lf)(y) = \sum_j f(τ_j(y))\,|τ_j'(y)|$, the sum over the branches $j$ of D whose image contains $y$. So factorial(m) * J[m] encloses the m-th derivative of $Lf$ at $y$.

For a circle map (PwMap{OnCircle}, see CircleMap) Y is read modulo 1, and an interval that crosses $0 \sim 1$ is split there, the jets of the pieces joined by hull. A branch contributes when Y is certified to lie in its image and not when certified to lie outside; otherwise an ArgumentError is thrown, and Y should be split at the endpoints of the branch images. f must be of class $C^k$ on the preimages and accept Taylor1 with interval coefficients; each branch of D must be of class $C^{k+1}$ and accept the same. The arithmetic is that of the interval type of Y; a real Y is read as a thin Interval{Float64}.

The argument, for one branch and one piece of Y. X = preimage(Y, br) contains $x_0 = τ(y)$ for every $y \in Y$. Expanding the branch on Taylor1([X, 1], k + 1) gives intervals that contain $T^{(m)}(x_0)/m!$; with the constant term set to zero this encloses the coefficients of $S(s) = T(x_0 + s) - T(x_0)$. Its series inverse $σ(t) = τ(y + t) - τ(y)$ is computed by TaylorSeries' inverse, the Lagrange formula $σ_m = [s^{m-1}](s/S)^m / m$: ring operations, a division by $S'(0) = T'(x_0) \ne 0$ and divisions by integers, so each coefficient of $σ$ encloses the true one. The jet of $f$ on Taylor1([X, 1], k) encloses $f^{(m)}(x_0)/m!$; composing it with $σ$ truncated at order $k$ and multiplying by $\pm σ'$ (the sign of the branch) gives the jet of $f(τ(y+t))\,|τ'(y+t)|$ to order $k$, using $f$ to order $k$ and $T$ to order $k + 1$. These are polynomial operations on enclosures of quantities that, for each $y$, are the true ones at $x_0 = τ(y)$, so the result encloses the true coefficients for every $y \in Y$.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.transfer_taylor_model — Function
transfer_taylor_model(D::PwMap, f, I::Interval, k; ϵ = 0.0, max_iter = 100,
                      newton_steps = 20) -> TaylorModels.TaylorModel1

A Taylor model $(P, Δ)$ of order k of $Lf$ on the interval I, expanded at the midpoint $y_0$ of I: $(Lf)(y) - P(y - y_0) \in Δ$ for every $y \in I$. Defined in the TaylorModels extension; load it with using TaylorModels. The hypotheses on D, f and I are those of transfer_jet with Y = I, with I inside $[0,1]$ also for a circle map; f and the branches must also accept real TaylorModel1 arguments (TaylorModels has + - * / and inv, sqrt, exp, log, sin, cos, tan, asin, acos, atan, sinh, cosh, tanh on them).

The argument, for one branch $T$ with inverse $τ$ on I. Start from the model with polynomial $p_0 = \operatorname{mid}X$ and remainder $Δ_0 = X - \operatorname{mid}X$, X = preimage(I, br), which contains $τ$. With a constant $c$ (an enclosure of $1/T'(\operatorname{mid}X)$, a free choice) and $N(g) = g - c\,(T∘g - \mathrm{id})$, the true $τ$ satisfies $τ = N(τ)$. If $τ - p \in Δ$ on I, the mean value theorem gives, for each $y$, $N(τ)(y) - N(p)(y) = (1 - c\,T'(ξ))\,(τ(y) - p(y))$ with $ξ$ between $τ(y)$ and $p(y)$, so in the hull $R$ of the ranges of $(p, Δ)$ and of $p$ on I. Hence $τ - p' \in Δ_Q + K Δ$, where $(p', Δ_Q)$ is the model of $N(p)$ formed by TaylorModels and $K = 1 - c\,T'(R)$, $T'(R)$ enclosed by an interval Taylor series. This step is repeated newton_steps times, or until $Δ$ stops shrinking; every iterate encloses $τ$. Then $|τ'| = \pm 1/T'(τ)$: the model of $T'∘τ$ is TaylorModels' rpa of $T'$ on the model of $τ$, with $T'$ acting on a series $u$ as $(T∘\tilde u)'/\tilde u'$, $\tilde u$ being $u$ extended by one zero coefficient (exact whenever $u'(0) \ne 0$, as for the argument $x_0 + s$ that rpa passes), and its reciprocal is inv of that model. The model of the branch's term is $f(τ)\,(\pm 1/T'(τ))$, and the terms are summed.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.transfer_taylor_models — Function
transfer_taylor_models(D::PwMap, f, I::Interval, k; ϵ = 0.0, max_iter = 100,
                       newton_steps = 20, parts = 1)
    -> Vector{NamedTuple{(:piece, :cells)}}

Taylor models of $Lf$ on I, organised by the cuts where $Lf$ may jump. Defined in the TaylorModels extension. I is cut at the endpoints of the branch images that lie inside it; the result lists the pieces between cuts in order, covering I, and each piece $K$ is covered by cells, a list of (piece, model) in order: on each cell $C$, $(Lf)(y) - P(y - y_C) \in Δ$ for every $y$ in the interior of $C$, $y_C$ the midpoint of $C$. Each $K$ is partitioned into parts equal cells, whose endpoints are free choices and not cuts: $Lf$ is continuous across them when $f$ is.

An endpoint of a branch image is an interval enclosure $E$. When $E$ is thin it is a cut point. When it is not, the sliver $E \cap I$ is a piece of its own with cells = nothing: whether that branch contributes is not decided there, and $Lf$ is not modelled on it. On every other piece the set of contributing branches is the same at every interior point, and the model of a cell is the sum of the branch models of transfer_taylor_model on it. At a cut that is the endpoint of a branch image the value of $Lf$ is not claimed by either neighbouring piece.

RigorousInvariantMeasures.transfer_taylor_models — Method
transfer_taylor_models(D::PwMap, F::AbstractVector{<:NamedTuple{(:piece, :cells)}},
                       I::Interval, k; ϵ = 0.0, max_iter = 100, newton_steps = 20,
                       parts = 1)

The same for a piecewise $f$ given in that form, for instance the output of a previous call, so that the call can be iterated. F is read as: $f$ is continuous on each piece with cells, and on each cell $f(x) - p_q(x - x_q) \in Δ_q$ for every $x$ in the interior of the cell, $x_q$ the expansion point of its model, of order k; on the pieces with cells = nothing and at the endpoints of the pieces, $f$ is not given.

The cuts are the branch image endpoints and, for each branch, the images $T(c)$ of the endpoints $c$ of the pieces of F that lie in its domain. On a piece $K$ of the output that is not a sliver no $T(c)$ lies in the interior, so each contributing branch maps the interior of $K$ into the interior of the one piece of F that contains $τ$ of the midpoint of $K$ (it is connected, contains that point and meets no $c$). If that piece has no cells, $K$ gets none; if no piece of F contains it, an ArgumentError is thrown.

The cells of $K$ are bounded by the points of its partition into parts equal intervals and by the images $T(c')$ of the interior cell boundaries $c'$ of those pieces of F. On a cell $C$ that contains no $T(c')$, $τ$ maps its interior into the interior of one input cell, found from $τ$ of the midpoint, and $f∘τ$ is enclosed by that cell's polynomial evaluated on the model of $τ - x_q$, plus $Δ_q$. When $T(c')$ is a non-thin enclosure, the cell $C$ it gives is enclosed by a constant model: $f∘τ$ on $C$ lies in the hull of the ranges of the input cells that $τ(C)$ meets, $f$ being continuous at $c'$, and $|τ'|$ in $1/|T'|$ on the preimage of $C$.
